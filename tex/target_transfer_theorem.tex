\section{Certified transfer across two fixed synthetic objectives}
\label{sec:target-transfer}
The earlier experiments compare scores from two manually selected sets of
two-dimensional target vectors. Here we study \emph{fixed-target transfer},
not statistical generalization to independently sampled tasks. For
prompts $p$ in the finite space $P_m$, let $F(p)=J_T(p)$ denote the
development objective and $G(p)=J_H(p)$ the held-out synthetic objective.
Define the signed shift $g(p)=G(p)-F(p)$ and its finite-space oscillation
\[
\Omega_m=\max_{p\in P_m}g(p)-\min_{p\in P_m}g(p).
\]
Both maxima and minima are computed by complete enumeration of the same
small spaces used in the existing oracle.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-objective transfer of simple regret]
\label{prop:target-transfer}
For any nonempty finite candidate set $P_m$, define
$R_F(p)=\max_{q\in P_m}F(q)-F(p)$, and define $R_G$ analogously.
Then for every $p\in P_m$,
\begin{equation}
 |R_G(p)-R_F(p)|\leq\Omega_m.
 \label{eq:regret-transfer}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, if $F(p)-F(q)>\Omega_m$ for two candidates, then $G(p)>G(q)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any $p$,
\[
\begin{split}
R_G(p)
 &=\max_q[F(q)+g(q)]-F(p)-g(p)\\
 &\leq \max_qF(q)-F(p)+\max_qg(q)-\min_qg(q)\\
 &= R_F(p)+\Omega_m.
\end{split}
\]
Exchanging $F$ and $G$ changes $g$ to $-g$, which has the same
oscillation, and yields the reverse inequality. For the ordering claim,
\[
 G(p)-G(q)
 =F(p)-F(q)+g(p)-g(q)
 \geq F(p)-F(q)-\Omega_m>0.
\]
No convexity assumption or gradient calculation is required.
\end{proof}

The previous result uses the enumerated gap. An independent
\emph{analytic upper bound} follows from the logistic link. Let $T$
and $H$ each contain $K$ vectors. Since
$|\sigma'(z)|\leq1/4$, for any permutation $\pi$ of the indices,
\[
 |J_T(x)-J_H(x)|
 \leq\frac{3}{4K}\|x\|_2
       \sum_{j=1}^K\|u_j-v_{\pi(j)}\|_2.
\]
Choose the permutation with smallest mean vector distance
$D(T,H)=\min_\pi K^{-1}\sum_j\|u_j-v_{\pi(j)}\|_2$.
The embedding definition in Equation~\eqref{eq:embedding} implies
\[
 \|x(p)\|_2\leq
 R_m=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m(1+0.1(i-1))
                  \max_{t\in\Vocab}\|E(t)\|_2.
\]
Consequently, for every length-$m$ prompt,
\begin{equation}
 |g(p)|\leq\frac34R_mD(T,H),\qquad
 \Omega_m\leq\frac32R_mD(T,H).
 \label{eq:target-shift-analytic}
\end{equation}
The permutation minimizes an \emph{upper bound}, not the observed score
discrepancy, and the vector pairing is an analytical device rather than
an empirical semantic matching claim.

The development optimum is the repeated abstract token label
\texttt{formal}, whereas the held-out optimum is the repeated label
\texttt{verify}, at each enumerated length. At lengths two, three and
four, the held-out regret of the development optimum is approximately
$0.057181$, $0.057159$ and $0.056911$ respectively. The labels are
arbitrary names of fixed vectors, not evidence that either English
word is an effective instruction for real LLMs.

Among pairs with distinct development scores, the held-out objective
reverses $149$, $5,487$ and $205,554$ pairwise rankings for the three
lengths. The sufficient ordering certificate preserves $64$, $940$
and $14,374$ pairwise orderings respectively, with no observed
certificate failures. A ranking not meeting the sufficient condition
is \emph{undecided} by the theorem, not necessarily reversed.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and numerical scope.}
Running \texttt{python3 src/target\_transfer\_audit.py} regenerates
\texttt{results/target\_transfer\_audit.json} and
\texttt{tex/target\_transfer\_audit.tex}. All $6^m$ candidates and
their unordered pairs are checked, using binary floating-point
evaluation and an improvement threshold of $10^{-12}$, rather than
exact symbolic evaluation of sigmoid values. The propositions are
analytic, while individual counts depend on the fixed benchmark
and numerical tolerance. This is a negative-result-aware,
claim-to-code audit following the methodological spirit of
\emph{A Society of AI Scientists}~\cite{ahmad2026}, not a result
borrowed from that study. These observations cannot support
real-model transfer claims without a separately controlled study.
